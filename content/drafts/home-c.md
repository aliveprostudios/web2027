# Home Page Content, Version C

Copy only. Nine sections.

**The strategy behind C, in one paragraph.** A and B open on the problem, which is what
every agency does. Tension is not belief, and the brief here is a visitor who sees their
return. Return is an arithmetic feeling, so C leads with proof and makes the reader do
sums. The spine is the retention figure, reframed: seventy percent of clients past sixteen
years is not a loyalty statistic, it is sixteen consecutive years of a business auditing
its own numbers and deciding the partnership pays. The second idea is the coordination tax,
the cost that sits between suppliers rather than on their invoices. Both are things no
competitor can copy, because one is a fact about Alive Pro and the other is a fact about
the reader.

## 2. The arithmetic of staying

**H2:** Retention is not a loyalty statistic. It is a return statistic.

Marketing partners get replaced. Budgets get audited, results get questioned, and the
agency that cannot show its worth is gone by the next fiscal year. That is normal, and it
is healthy.

Which is what makes a sixteen-year relationship worth a second look. It means a business
sat down with its own numbers, year after year, and concluded every single time that the
partnership returned more than it cost. Seventy percent of our clients have reached that
conclusion more than sixteen times each.

That is the only endorsement that matters, and it is the one thing an agency cannot
manufacture.

## 3. The coordination tax

**H2:** Six vendors is not six relationships. It is fifteen.

Count the suppliers running your brand right now. Strategy. Design. Website. Video. Social.
Ads. Content. Each one sends an invoice you can see.

What you cannot see is the cost between them. Six suppliers means fifteen pairs who have to
agree on what the brand is, what it sounds like, and who it is for. Add a seventh and it
becomes twenty-one. Nobody budgets for those relationships. You pay for them anyway.

- **Every handoff is a re-brief,** and a re-brief is the same work bought twice.
- **Six interpretations produce six brands.** The market averages them into one blurred impression, and blurred brands compete on price.
- **Each campaign starts from zero,** because the last one belonged to somebody else.
- **Your own hours go into coordination** instead of the business, and your hours are the most expensive line on the page.

This is the coordination tax. It appears on no invoice, which is precisely why it never
gets cut.

## 8. The first ninety days

**H2:** What happens between the call and the work

**The conversation.** No pitch and no deck. We ask what is working, what is not, and what
prompted you to look. By the end both of us know whether there is a fit, and we will say so
either way.

**Discovery and audit.** Competitive intelligence, a review of your digital presence, an
assessment of your sales process, and conversations with your leadership team. You get an
honest read on where the brand actually stands, which is useful to you whatever you decide
next.

**Strategy and proposal.** Not a menu of services with prices attached. A recommendation
mapping specific work to the outcomes you want, with goals and KPIs defined before anything
begins, and a ninety-day action plan.

**Then the work.** The same people throughout. Defined milestones and dates. Reporting tied
to outcomes rather than to activity.

## Strategic notes, read before choosing C

**1. C is load-bearing on one number, and it has to hold.** The whole page rests on "70% of
our clients have been with us for over 16 years", which appears today in
`content/pages/partnership.md` and `content/pages/about-us.md`. On those pages it is a
supporting line. In C it is the argument. Before this ships, confirm the figure is
defensible from actual client records, because a visitor who doubts it loses the entire
page, and a competitor who challenges it costs you more than the claim was worth. If it
turns out to be an estimate, C still works with softer framing, for example "most of our
clients have been with us longer than a decade", but it should be your call, not an
assumption.

**2. The strongest ROI device you own is currently switched off.** Precision Impact Sprints
is a $550 session, ninety minutes, credited back in full if the client engages you. That is
textbook risk reversal and it is the single most return-legible thing on the site, which is
why it was unpublished for copy revision rather than for lack of merit. C closes on the free
consultation because that is what is live today. My recommendation is that the Sprint comes
back and becomes C's secondary CTA, replacing "See the Work". A visitor who is not ready to
commit but can see a fee returned in full is a visitor who converts. The "ninety-day plan is
yours either way" line in section 8 is the same instinct applied to the free path, and it
needs your approval because it is a promise, not a description.

**3. Section 4 features one client's revenue outcome.** The Kabinetry quote is already
public on `/alive-pro/testimonials`, so re-using it breaks nothing. Featuring a doubled
sales figure at the top of the home page is a bigger spotlight than a testimonial page
gives it. Worth a courtesy note to Fawad Mirza, and worth checking whether any of the four
key projects can supply an outcome number of their own. One real number from Alfred Locks,
Gallery Specialty, ASSA ABLOY or Claritas would do more for this page than any sentence I
can write.

**4. The fifteen relationships figure is arithmetic, not rhetoric.** Six suppliers form
fifteen pairs, seven form twenty-one, by n(n-1)/2. It is checkable, which is exactly why it
persuades. Do not let anyone round it into "dozens of relationships" during design.

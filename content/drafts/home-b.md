## 2. Fragmented marketing

**H2:** Fragmented marketing is the default. It is also the problem.

How many vendors does it take to run your brand? One for strategy. One for design. One for
the website. One for video. One for social. One for ads. One for content. Each is
competent. Each is working from a different brief. And you are the only person who sees all
of it at once.

Nobody sets out to fragment a brand. It happens through reasonable decisions made one at a
time, over years. A designer for the logo. An agency for the campaign. A developer for the
site. A freelancer to keep the blog moving. Every hire made sense on its own. Together they
produced a brand assembled from parts that were never designed to fit.

You can usually spot it:

- Your website, your sales deck, and your social feed each describe the business differently.
- Every vendor optimizes their own slice. Nobody owns the result.
- Work gets redone because the next supplier never had the last one's context.
- Your team spends more time coordinating suppliers than building the business.
- When results slip, nobody can tell you why.

---

## 3. What it costs

**H2:** The cost is not the invoices. It is the interference.

Fragmentation never appears as a line item. It appears as spend that refuses to compound.

- **Budget leaks in the gaps.** Every handoff is a re-brief, and every re-brief is paid for twice.
- **Positioning drifts.** Six vendors interpreting a brand produce six versions of it, and the market averages them into confusion.
- **Momentum resets.** Each campaign starts from zero instead of building on the one before it.
- **Trust erodes quietly.** Buyers rarely name inconsistency. They just choose the brand that seems more certain.

Fragmented effort produces fragmented results. That is not a budget problem you can spend
your way out of. It is a structural one.

A rebrand changes how a business looks. A transformation changes how it performs.

We start where most agencies finish: with the strategy. Who you are, who you are for, what

That is the difference between marketing that costs money and marketing that compounds.

tile takes the first four by `order`: Web Solutions 1, Sales & Marketing Collateral 2,

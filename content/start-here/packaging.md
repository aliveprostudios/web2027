## Three Seconds, and the Print Bill

Your packaging is the only part of your brand a customer actually holds. It has to do the selling before a word gets read, and it has to do it at a cost that is not quietly eating the margin on every unit you ship.

Packaging goes wrong in one of two directions, and both cost the same to print. It looks good and prints badly, which surfaces as a quote you cannot accept or a colour you cannot hold across a run. Or it prints cleanly and says nothing, which is the more expensive failure, because you pay for the run either way. The reason is nearly always the same: the design was finished before anybody asked how it would be made.

We decide the structure, the substrate, the colour build and the finish alongside the artwork rather than after it. That is the part most studios hand off, and it is exactly where the money is. Building the colour properly, choosing a finish that survives the shelf, and designing to the sheet instead of against it routinely takes thousands out of a print run without changing how the pack looks.

> *"A packaging design that wins an award and cannot be printed at your volume is not a design. It is a proposal. The constraint is the brief."*

**Javad Ahmadi, Brand Transformation Architect**

## A Style Guide Will Not Fix This

Inconsistency is almost never a design problem. Most companies with a scattered brand already have a style guide. It is on a shared drive somewhere, and it was completely correct on the day it was made.

The cause is ownership. Sales needs a deck by Thursday and builds it themselves. Somebody's nephew handles the social. A trade show supplier designs the booth from a logo emailed at short notice. Nobody is doing anything wrong, and at no point does one person look at the whole thing together. Five people commissioning work from four suppliers produces five brands, and the market reads that as a company not quite in control of itself.

We fix it in two moves. Build a system that is genuinely usable by the people making things at short notice, rather than a document that assumes a designer is available. Then hold the line as work goes out the door, which is the unglamorous part that actually keeps it consistent a year from now.

> *"Consistency is not a design deliverable. It is a habit with somebody responsible for it. Without that, every guideline document becomes a historical record."*

**Javad Ahmadi, Brand Transformation Architect**

Put your website, your last sales deck and your social feed side by side on one screen. Whatever you feel looking at those three is the brief.
